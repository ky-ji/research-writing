# Blog Pre-Publish Checklist

Go through every box before publishing. Report failures to the user with the fix applied.

## Idea And Structure

- [ ] The one sentence (at most 20 words) exists and the TL;DR's first line states it.
- [ ] The post contains a genuine surprise (before-belief versus after-belief).
- [ ] Reading only the title, TL;DR, headings, and figure takeaways gives the full argument.
- [ ] Every section heading is a claim rather than a topic label.
- [ ] Every figure and table maps to exactly one claim, and orphan evidence has been cut or moved to `<details>`.
- [ ] The hook is concrete (one scene or one number) rather than background history.
- [ ] Competing explanations and confounds are addressed where the claim is made.
- [ ] Multi-model comparisons are framed by architectural paradigm rather than bare checkpoint names, stating both what is invariant and what varies systematically along the structural axis.
- [ ] No standalone *Limitations* section (scope caveats live concisely inline next to claims, and experimental protocol details live in `<details>`).
- [ ] Closing answers the title question in plain words with no duplicate recap list.

## Prose

- [ ] Paragraphs have at most 4 sentences with one idea each.
- [ ] Every term is defined at first use with one canonical name throughout.
- [ ] No code identifiers appear in running prose (only in tables, `<details>`, or code blocks).
- [ ] At most 2 numbers per sentence and 4 per paragraph (tables excluded).
- [ ] No self-praising adjectives (`striking`, `remarkable`, `dramatic`, `zero-loophole`, `best-paper`).
- [ ] No hedge stacks (`may potentially suggest`) and no throat-clearing (`It is worth noting that`).
- [ ] Active voice and concrete subjects ("the head contracts" instead of "contraction is observed").
- [ ] Global punctuation habit followed everywhere: zero em-dashes (`—` or `——`) in prose, headings, titles, captions, or tables, and no colons, semicolons, or trailing colons in newly drafted prose/headings (preserve code/KaTeX/BibTeX syntax).
- [ ] Transitions are questions or short bridges rather than "Next, we...".
- [ ] Word count cut by at least 25 percent in the edit pass compared to the first draft.

## Figures

- [ ] Figure 1 explains the idea without text.
- [ ] Each figure has title and takeaway above and caption below.
- [ ] Consistent color semantics with at most 2 accents, declared once.
- [ ] Direct labels, legends with at most 3 items, no code names, and real subscripts.
- [ ] No label or legend collisions (verified by screenshot at 1.5x).
- [ ] Figures generated by a script from archived data.

## Rigor

- [ ] Every number matches the source JSON or logs.
- [ ] Rounding is identical across text, figures, and tables.
- [ ] N, seeds, and environment are stated in captions or `<details>`.
- [ ] Oracle, upper-bound, or simulation-only caveats are stated concisely inline next to the claim (no standalone *Limitations* section).
- [ ] Claims do not exceed evidence (for example, "matches" versus "beats" given N = 16).

## Visual And HTML

- [ ] Renders cleanly at 1440px and true 390px (iframe check) with no horizontal page overflow.
- [ ] KaTeX renders with no raw `$` left, and CSS selectors do not break KaTeX spans (avoid styling bare `span` inside text blocks).
- [ ] TOC entries are short and numbered, and active-section highlight works.
- [ ] Reading time and date appear in the byline, and the post is listed on the blog index and homepage.
- [ ] HTML parses cleanly and SVGs are valid XML.
