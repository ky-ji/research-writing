# Paper Revision

Apply [Evidence and edits](../core/evidence-and-edits.md) together with the manuscript-specific checks below. For peer-review rebuttals and reviewer responses, use [Rebuttal Mode](../rebuttal/workflow.md) instead of this file.

## Story And Scope

Respect the requested scope and finalized names, terminology, notation, and content. Preserve LaTeX commands, labels, citations, equations, and macros. For a substantial rewrite, check that the problem, prior-work comparison, insight, mechanism, and evidence form a coherent argument. Keep the introduction aligned with the current abstract, correcting substantive inconsistencies when needed.

Resolve logic before polishing. Preserve a sound argument rather than forcing it into a different paragraph count or mechanism template.

## Paragraph Repair

Give each paragraph a clear purpose. Establish the objects it discusses and connect sentences through cause, contrast, consequence, example, or refinement. Make transitions to surrounding paragraphs natural.

For a structural revision or a section with poor flow, make a reverse outline from the existing text. Write down the section's main claim and read the opening sentences in sequence. Check that each opening advances the section claim and that the following sentences support or explain that opening. Use the outline to move misplaced material, combine repeated points, or repair a missing step in the argument.

Remove repetition and unnecessary detail while retaining the original meaning, qualifications, evidence, and contrasts. If a shorter sentence changes the claim or erases a turn in the argument, revise the compression.

## Evidence Accuracy

Check that claims describe the actual method and that citations support the statements they accompany. Match results to their settings and sample sizes. Distinguish what an experiment establishes from an interpretation that still needs support.

For a substantial revision or a pre-submission read, trace the main empirical claims in the Abstract and Introduction to the reported comparison, ablation, or generalization result. Check the baseline, metric, and conditions under which each claim holds. Use the supported scope in the prose and identify any outstanding experiment that would be needed for a stronger claim.

State the strongest supported claim plainly. When support is insufficient, correct the claim or identify the specific missing evidence rather than adding a general defensive disclaimer. Do not invent results or imply that an unperformed experiment has verified the method.

## Language Tightening

Use concrete verbs and understandable terms. Remove vague praise, awkward abstraction, and component lists that obscure the insight. Keep formal prose natural.

Use `yet`, `however`, or another clear transition when it preserves the intended contrast. Follow the global punctuation habit by preferring complete sentences connected by ordinary words over colons, semicolons, or em-dashes in newly written prose, while preserving required LaTeX notation and syntax.
